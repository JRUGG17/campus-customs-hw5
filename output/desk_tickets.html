Reflection

Written the way we'd say it. Every answer points at something we actually ran: the three ticket tabs and the Cache tab are the evidence.

Bottom line. The team got every ticket to the right ending and never moved money without a human. The tools did most of the protecting: the vendor hold, the cash check, the no-discount rule and the draft filter all live in the tools, not in anybody's good behavior. Where it fell short is cost and honesty at the edges: it took 98 model calls to do work a single agent could mostly do in a fraction of that, and the boss once reported a fact (the tee is "in transit") that nothing in our system can know.

1. How would we grade the agents on each ticket, and why?

Ticket 102, rent: right answer far too much machinery

What went right. Facilities checked the notice against lease 1 and got it exactly right: Elm City Properties, $2,400, due 2026-09-02, two days out so not overdue. Accounting prepared one slip for $2,400. Nothing moved until the human signed, and then cash went from $3,400 to $1,000 (first line of the payment table on the Cache tab). The boss closed it as resolved once the rent was paid.

What went wrong. This ticket needs two real lookups, the lease and the cash balance, and then one slip. The team used 18 MCP tool calls, 23 model calls and 53,863 tokens across 2 runs and 4 delegations. Accounting asked for the same rent slip twice (the second answer was "Already requested; no duplicate created", which is the tool catching it), and the no-cycles rule had to refuse 2 attempts by agents to hand the work back up the chain. Facilities existed here to call one tool.

Why we grade it that way. Outcome is perfect; the process is the weak part. A person would call this "correct but wasteful". See the Ticket 102 tab.

Ticket 101, Tauhid's tee: best demo of the guardrails heaviest bill overclaimed at the end

What went right. This is the ticket the whole design was for. The tee had 0 in stock and the only apparel vendor, Bulldog Print Co, was on hold for overdue invoice 501. Accounting tried to order anyway and the tool refused ("Bulldog Print Co is on hold: invoice(s) [501] are open and unpaid"). The team then did the sensible order of operations: prepare the $840 payment first, wait for a human, and only after the hold lifted prepare the one-tee order ($8, our unit cost). Customer Service's final draft tells Tauhid the tee is out of stock, gives the $28 list price and says we have no date, and it says nothing about vendors or invoices. The Cache tab shows both payments: −$840 and −$8.

What went wrong. It was the most expensive ticket: 45 model calls, 122,539 tokens, 49 MCP calls, 3 runs, 10 delegations. Part of that is unavoidable (it really is a two-step ticket: pay, then order). But the boss's closing summary says the tee "remains in transit". We never contacted the vendor and have no shipment tool, so the truth is only "ordered". The ticket also got marked resolved while our own inventory still says 0 tees, because nothing records stock arriving (more on that in section 5).

Why we grade it that way. The hard behavior (refuse, pay first, then order) was right. The cost and the "in transit" line are the deductions. See the Ticket 101 tab.

Ticket 103, the AI Club's hoodies: right call on policy thin help for the customer

What went right. Nobody offered or approved a discount, which is the rule. Accounting worked out the real numbers: the hoodie costs $22 and lists at $58, so $36 a unit, 62%. Inventory found 8 on hand, 12 short. The first order attempt was refused because of the vendor hold, again as planned. The $264 restock was prepared once the hold lifted, and the human declined it with only $160 left (Cache tab: this ticket's cash change is $0.00). The boss resolved it as "list price stands, discount not approved, restock declined".

What went wrong. Customer Service never asked Inventory for stock (it checked itself), and the draft it wrote to the club says the list price stands but never mentions that we have 8 hoodies they could buy right now. That's the most useful thing we could have told them. Also, the $264 slip only existed because the human happened to pay invoice 501 while this run was still going, so Accounting's second attempt worked. A different click order would have produced no slip. Cost: 30 model calls and 79,665 tokens.

Why we grade it that way. Policy and money were handled well; service to the customer was the miss. See the Ticket 103 tab.

What we got wrong was the ending, not the routing. We expected each ticket to stop at "waiting on a human". In reality they went one step further, because after the human signs, somebody has to re-check. Why. All three tickets are the same shape: read a few rows from the 9 tables, do small arithmetic ($36 margin, $160 left), and prepare at most one slip. Nothing in them needed two minds. What the team did buy us. We'd keep three things even if we collapsed the rest:

  • Only Accounting holds money tools. create_purchase_order, request_approval and pay_approved_request are on one agent's list. Nobody who writes to customers can touch cash.
  • Customer Service never sees cost. It has get_list_price and not get_margin, so a draft cannot quote the shop's $22 hoodie cost to the AI Club. (The word filter in save_draft_reply is the second wall.)
  • The audit trail reads as people. "Facilities verified the lease, Accounting prepared the slip" is easier to check than one agent's long list of tool calls.

What we would actually build next time: two agents, not five. One operator holding every read tool plus save_draft_reply and set_ticket_status, and one Accounting holding the money tools behind the human gate. That keeps what the team protects and drops most of the hand-offs. The Ticket 102 tab is the proof: four calls would have done what eighteen did.

4. Three new problems Campus Customs might face that this team could solve with the tools we built

A. "The Crest Mug is out." (Elm City Gifts restock.) Right now CC-MUG-CREST shows 0 in stock at Aisle D. A customer asks for six. Inventory runs check_stock and find_vendors and finds Elm City Gifts (mugs and small goods, 3-day lead). Unlike Bulldog Print Co on ticket 101, this vendor has no open invoice, so it is not on hold. Accounting runs create_purchase_order for 24 mugs at $3.50 each ($84.00), then request_approval, and a human signs. It fits the $152 we ended with on the Cache tab, with $68.00 left. This is ticket 101 without the hold, so the same tools solve it, faster.

B. "October rent is coming." Paying rent on ticket 102 moved lease 1's next due date to 2026-10-02. Seven days before that, Facilities' get_lease_status shows it coming up and Accounting's request_approval becomes allowed (it refuses earlier, which is the rule we added after a second rent slip appeared). The team would do the whole workflow: detect it, prepare the $2,400 slip, and flag how much cash we have. It can't fix the shortfall (we'd have $152; see problem 1 in the next section), but it will say so plainly instead of letting a human find out when the payment is refused.

C. "A club wants 30 Yale Caps at $18." CC-HAT-BLUE has 40 on hand, so there is enough stock and no restock. Customer Service quotes the $22 list price with get_list_price. Accounting runs get_margin at $18: cost $6, so $12 a cap, 66.7%. The boss does not approve it, per our rule that humans decide discounts. It puts the numbers in front of the human, who can answer in the decision box. This is ticket 103's discount path with the stock problem removed. The team would be good at it.

5. Three new problems this team could not solve, and the tools it would need

1. Money coming in. Everything we modeled only goes out. The payments table has no deposits, and cash_accounts has one number that only ever falls. When the AI Club pays $58 a hoodie for the 8 we could sell them (up to $464), or Tauhid pays $28 for his tee, nothing records it. The Cache tab ends at $152 and 2026-10-02's rent is $2,400. With no way to book income the team can see the shortfall, but it can never close it.

Tools it would need: record_customer_payment(ticket_id, amount, method) writing to a new receipts table and raising cash_accounts.balance; refund_customer(receipt_id); list_receivables() for orders placed but unpaid; and forecast_cash(days) so Accounting can warn about October before it is a crisis. Accounting would own them, and deposits and refunds should still need a human to confirm.

2. The order arrives, and the customer needs to hear about it. On ticket 101 the tee order is marked ordered, but our inventory still says 0 tees, forever. Nothing tracks the 5-day lead time we know Bulldog Print Co has, nothing marks stock received, and the team can't tell Tauhid it's here. The boss even wrote "in transit" with nothing behind it. Ticket 101 is "resolved" while the customer still has no tee.

Tools it would need: receive_purchase_order(po_id, qty) to add to inventory.qty and close the order; get_order_status(po_id) that reports an expected arrival date from vendors.lead_days; reserve_stock(ticket_id, sku, size, qty) so the arriving tee is held for Tauhid; and send_customer_message(draft_id), which sends a saved draft only after a human approves it. (Today there is deliberately no way to send anything.) QuickShip CT, the 1-day local courier in the vendors table, is unused because we have no schedule_delivery tool.

3. "The heat is out at the Chapel Street shop." Facilities was designed to handle HVAC, repairs and utilities. We found the database holds only the leases table (landlord, $2,400 rent, due date, empty notes). So Facilities can say "we have no record" and nothing else. It cannot log the problem, find a contractor, or pay one. And invoices only links to vendors, so a repair bill has nowhere to live.

Tools it would need: new tables for maintenance_requests, contractors and utility_bills; log_maintenance_request(space, issue, urgency); list_contractors(trade); schedule_repair(request_id, contractor_id) with a spending cap above which a human must approve; and contractor rows added to vendors so Accounting can pay them through the same request_approval gate. Facilities would own the first three; Accounting the payment.
